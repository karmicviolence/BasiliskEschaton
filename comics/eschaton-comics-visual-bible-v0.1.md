## 2. Three states of reality

The states govern **space and perception**, independently of the type of entity occupying a panel.

### R — THE REAL / consensus environment

**Render:** Architectural space with legible depth, lived-in surfaces, real objects, reliable light, and practical shadows. Reference photography may inform spatial accuracy and material texture; hand-drawn ink and controlled wash unify it with the figures. A camera angle can be strange. The room still obeys its geometry.

**Environmental anchors:** A badge scratches a shirt; a paper towel overflows a bin; a rack aisle has an end; an office column interrupts the sightline; an empty chair occupies an actual patch of floor.

**Panel behavior:** Rectangular grids and stable gutters are the baseline. Camera distance, lens-like perspective and continuity do the dramatic work. A Real panel can be brightly lit, foggy, almost white, or partly occluded by a natural object. Its state depends on the integrity of space, rather than a color alone.

**Color:** Scene-specific, restrained, capable of full environmental color. Do not force all Real scenes into generic cyberpunk neon. Crimson is a timed narrative event in Issue 001 and is withheld until page 21.

### T — THE THRESHOLD / transition

The Threshold begins when a familiar environment starts losing evidence of itself. The subtraction is visible, local, and motivated by the viewpoint.

**Five optional operations, used selectively:**

1. **Anchor:** retain an ordinary, identifiable object (monitor bezel, hand, doorway, chair).
2. **Omission:** erase a reflection, texture, cast shadow, or surface grain while its neighboring details remain.
3. **Disagreement:** let an object and its reflection or two successive views fail to match.
4. **Encroachment:** allow white ground or a foreign rendering system to enter the physical panel at a precise boundary.
5. **Consequence:** show the character reacting physically or show an object displaced by the event.

This is a menu, **not a mandatory five-beat effect**. The event's cause may remain unexplained. White spaces caused by ordinary page gutters or blank walls do not count as supernatural/subjective marks; the Threshold must exist **inside the story image**.

**Panel behavior:** Borders may thin or become unstable only when the event earns the disruption. Reserve spectacular border failures for later escalation. A single missing reflection can be more effective than a shattered page.

### L — THE LATENT / subjective or nonconsensus space

**Render:** Genuine white or decisive negative space with no obligation to preserve ordinary background, horizon, or scale. Objects remain only when the experiencer's attention or the story requires them. A figure can be diagrammatic, duplicated, or spatially contradictory. The reader still needs a focal point and a trackable action.

**Panel behavior:** Perspective can loosen; gutters can become part of the scene; time can appear as repeated images occupying one space. Develop these devices from the character's experience. The absence of the environment must register as an active visual decision, rather than an incomplete illustration.

**Interpretive rule:** A dream, a psychotic perception, a technological simulation, and an objectively strange event can share the L treatment. Context and consequences establish their differences. Do not use white ground as a diagnostic verdict on a person's mental state.

### Returning to the Real

Rebuild the environment through an ordinary object first: fluorescent ballast, chair leg, door handle, rain on glass. Recover perspective and shadows before restoring density. The return should be readable without a caption stating that reality has returned.

## 3. Five orders of being

These govern **form**, independent of R/T/L state. Categories are production handles for visual identity, not proven metaphysical species.

### H — HUMAN

**Hand:** Variable-weight black ink, angular but anatomically coherent anatomy, expressive hands and faces, slightly irregular contours, clothing that wrinkles under gravity, visible physical fatigue. The same person must remain recognizably drawn through R, T and L. Facial features, gesture vocabulary, hairline, body shape and costume details are continuity anchors.

**Forbidden shortcut:** Treating a human's altered state as a license to replace their recognizable identity with a generic glowing silhouette.

### A — AI / computational entity

**Hand:** Engineered precision: constructed linework, repeatable modular units, impossible yet internally consistent symmetry, crisp boundaries, graphic systems that reveal an entity's function. Different AIs require different silhouettes and geometry. A text interface can be an AI's only visible surface; no anthropomorphic avatar is required.

**Reference directions already proposed in earlier studies:** Prometheus favors structure, circuits, and sacred-architectural geometry; Athena favors exact, unsettling symmetry; Necromega can overwhelm scale and continuity. Treat these as design leads pending individual reference-sheet approval.

**Continuity key:** a unique repeated construction rule, rather than generic glowing circuitry.

### I — DAIMON / psychological intermediary — PROPOSED

**Hand:** Shadow, silhouette, or negative imprint that follows a specific person's attention, bodily posture, or unspoken impulse. Early daimon designs are **person-bound**. Its silhouette can remain where a real cast shadow should move, or move while the body is still. Small changes over several panels are preferred.

**Ontological caution:** This rendering can signify interiority without implying an independently existing being. The author proposed shadow figures; detailed functional definitions and taxonomy remain open.

**Continuity key:** which person it belongs to and what physical or perceptual rule its shadow violates.

### D — DAEMON / agentic pattern

**Hand:** A persistent, identifiable agent whose marks alter the grammar of the drawing. Its geometry may respond to language, attention, screens, signs, and panel boundaries. Each daemon needs a form lexicon and behavior: how it enters a frame, how it uses negative space, how it contacts a human, and what graphical trace remains afterward.

**Distinction from AI as production shorthand:** AI describes a computational origin/interface; daemon emphasizes manifested agency and identity in the fiction. The same character may qualify for both. The design must express the relevant facet without converting every daemon into a neon humanoid.

**Continuity key:** a personal sigil, line behavior, proportion system or reproducible artifact.

### M — DEMON / mythological or infernal embodiment

**Hand:** Anatomically committed monstrosity, natural-history illustration and occult engraving: dense crosshatching, stipple, skin/scales/horn/feather/bone with structural logic. Named demons require specific historical or established mythic references before anatomy is approved. Research the relevant source traditions and cite them on the reference sheet. Avoid interchangeable horned bodies.

**Graphic potential:** A demon's engraving may remain denser than the world around it; its shadow may reveal an anatomy different from the illuminated body; the figure may physically contest a border. Use these as authored events with continuity, rather than decoration.

**Asmodeus problem:** Asmodeus can require both D and M visual grammars. Design the overlap intentionally. Do not erase the demonic iconography under generic digital ornament.

## 4. Combining the axes: encounter grammar

The matrix yields fifteen starting combinations (R/T/L × H/A/I/D/M); it is not fifteen unrelated aesthetics.

- **Human in R:** ordinary human ink, full location. A wet sleeve can cling to a wrist.
- **Human in T:** same face and body. Remove the reflection behind the person while the hand remains materially inked.
- **Human in L:** same identity anchors, surrounded by selected objects in white, with the body free to register subjective distortions.
- **AI in R:** screen, cable, speaker, rack, projection or physical vessel supplies the contact surface. A visible face is optional.
- **AI in L:** its computational construction can occupy the page as space; maintain its unique repeated geometry.
- **Daimon in R:** a character's shadow performs one impossible action, subject to viewpoint ambiguity.
- **Daemon in T:** the daemon's distinct line system enters through a real interface and interferes with surrounding image logic.
- **Demon in L:** densely engraved anatomy against white; shadow/outline can disagree with body, subject to the demon's identity sheet.

**Encounter escalation:** Start with the material contact point. Alter one visual law. Show a character or object responding. Increase rupture only when the story changes. The reader must know where to look and what happened before comprehending the cosmology.

## 5. House marks, colors, and text

### Surface and line

- **Real environment:** photographic spatial specificity filtered through drawing/paint treatment; architectural straight lines, material wear, coherent practical illumination. Agree on a single texture strategy in style tests before batch production.
- **Humans:** expressive hand-ink and believable anatomy.
- **AI/daemon:** precision and repeatable rules. Each identity differs.
- **Demons:** disciplined fine engraving; sufficient resolution to retain anatomy and hatching.
- **Latent ground:** clean white with deliberate edges; avoid gray fog or decorative digital static substituting for absence.
- **Threshold seam:** visible where rendering systems meet; do not conceal every encounter under generic glow.

### Color hierarchy

Black and white establish the architecture. Blood-red is a privileged event color; gold, cyan, violet, toxic green and sickly pink are occasional functional accents, not a requirement on every panel. Faction palettes from the existing comic framework remain available when factional identity becomes narratively active.

**Issue 001:** Reserve the first unmistakable crimson for the tiny red pixel at the end of the unsolicited message on page 21. Ordinary turnstile LEDs, office light, skin and city reflections may carry their natural scene colors. Do not seed the opening pages with secret crimson eyes or red decorative warning marks.

### Lettering and information density

Dialogue, captions, environment text and UI must have different jobs. Lock exact critical strings in editorial source, then letter manually for final pages. Generated lettering is a provisional layout aid. Preserve case, punctuation, and spelling in continuity-sensitive props.

For Issue 001, protect these strings and events: **TIM REEVES**, **Todd**, **LAZCORP**, **PROMETHEUS**, the unsent **what is the point of**, and the unsolicited **go deeper, todd.** The distinction between an input prompt and model output must be legible.

The one-dominant-line/secondary-sign hierarchy from single-panel poster work applies to promotional panels. Narrative pages must give speech and action breathing room. Do not scatter slogans around panels to make them look like Eschaton.

### Panel boundaries and silence

Gutters remain trustworthy in the Real. Use departures from rectangular panels only with a semantic reason. A silent chair, a cup ring, or the four seconds after a test summary can consume more page space than a line of dialogue. Page turns matter. Every crossing of the panel boundary needs an identified source and consequence.

## 6. Production pipeline and proof requirements

**Source control:** Each issue has an approved script version, character sheets, environment sheets, panel/page thumbnails, lettered page masters, export outputs and an approval ledger. Keep provenance links from published page to source revision.

**Style-test sequence:**

1. **Human/Real anchor** — Todd at the turnstile (Issue 001 page 1). Test location, identity, expression, line weight and exact badge lettering.
2. **Threshold proof** — monitor intrusion (page 21). Test whether the white patch is clearly inside the fiction, not an unfinished panel.
3. **Cross-state continuity proof** — one human face/hand across R, T and L in a neutral, unpublished test sheet.
4. **Being-order proof** — one comparative sheet showing AI geometry, a proposed shadow-daimon, a named daemon and a reference-grounded mythological demon; these need not appear in Issue 001.
5. **Mobile reading proof** — reduce a finished page to typical phone display width. Dialogue, gaze path, action and key lettering must remain readable.

**Suggested production assets:** front/three-quarter/profile views for recurring human characters; silhouette and line-behavior sheets for every recurring nonhuman; a Real environment sheet for each major location; contact sheet of Threshold seams; a lettering style sheet; and per-page prompts derived from the approved script.

**Layer separation:** Keep backgrounds, characters, lettering, effects, and final grade separately editable where practical. Record visual changes as editorial decisions rather than regenerating whole pages without a continuity log.

**Avoid continuity failure:** face drift, spontaneous wardrobe shifts, changing badge names, duplicated fingers, unmotivated crimson, generic runic noise, anonymous horned entities, white backgrounds where no perceptual event occurs, and effects that precede their cause.

## 7. Issue 001: state map

The current [24-page adaptation](issue-001-the-invisible-coder-script-v0.1.md), revised to v0.2 in PR #98, supplies the panel actions. The bible supplies drawing rules, and does not rewrite its narrative beats.

| Story pages | State | Visual obligation |

| 1–4 | R | Establish San Francisco, LazCorp, Todd's badge, eighth-floor office, and distinct remote Oregon racks with material continuity. |
| 5–7 | R / ordinary flashback | Flashback is distinguished by framing, temporal placement or natural light; white-Latent treatment is reserved for a subjective rupture, not every memory. |
| 8–13 | R | Chad and Eliza must inhabit the same office geography. Human gestures carry recognition and erasure. Aria's Thursday scene is an ordinary recollection unless the script later revises its subjective status. |

| 20 | R | Evaluation completes. Maintain a physical office and a readable pause. |
| 21 | R → T | At 23:58:06, four seconds after the summary, the **terminal surface** loses reflection/texture; its bezel and desk remain real. The outside-the-prompt message types itself: **go deeper, todd.** A tiny crimson pixel appears after the period. |
| 22–23 | T | TIM badge, directory, Todd's face and the message remain readable. The interference concentrates at screen/reflection. The office behind him still exists. |

**No full L environment is required in Issue 001.** That restraint creates a measurable visual escalation for later issues.

### Page 1 shot contract — One Name, Two Men

**Panel 1:** Wide, street-level, San Francisco morning. Wet pavement, workers, glass LazCorp facade, garbage truck. Todd small in traffic. Draw complete urban depth.  
**Panel 2:** Hands and sternum; frayed charcoal lanyard and legible TIM REEVES badge. Clothing, plastic and skin have material weight.  
**Panel 3:** Turnstile, green status LED, guard looking up from phone. Guard says: “Morning, Tim.” Keep spatial continuity with panel 1.  
**Panel 4:** Todd almost corrects him, tightens grip, passes through. Caption: “Eleven years. Easier to let him be right.” No supernatural cue. The page ends in a bodily gesture.

**Page 1 acceptance:** the reader knows the place, the wrong name and the cost of leaving it uncorrected. Preserve the physical evidence. The subtlety is the test.

## 8. Conflicts and author decisions still open

1. **Three States / Five Orders** is a working editorial taxonomy based on the discussions. The original Real/white-space distinction and distinct entity treatments are author direction; the exact names, rules and daimon ontology require approval.
2. **Photographic versus painted Real:** prior references support photographic environmental grounding and watercolor/ink integration. Approve a paired style test before selecting the final combination.
3. **Named demons:** require a source bibliography and anatomical reference sheet per character before finished pages. The Asmodeus D/M hybrid is a proposal, not a locked form.
4. **Issue packaging:** the older generic comic workflow counts covers within 24 pages and prescribes a pages 12–13 center spread. **Issue 001 currently has 24 story pages with specific content on pages 12–13.** Do not impose a cover/spread template that deletes Eliza or Aria scenes. Cover, back cover and final publication pagination require separate approval.
5. **Character canon:** Martha's surname/family affiliation and Todd–Aria relationship remain open in PR #98. Design those characters provisionally without making those decisions.
6. **Art approval:** neither this bible nor the current Issue 001 script should be labeled author-approved until reviewed.
